The starting point

Why wealth matters

Income shows what a family earns in a year. Wealth — what a family owns minus what it owes — shows whether they can withstand a shock, invest in the future, and pass opportunity forward. Mass ECHOS is the first statewide measure of that difference in Massachusetts, and it reframes what economic security actually requires.

35.4% of Massachusetts families could not cover an unexpected $400 expense Mass ECHOS, p. 20
15.7% hold zero or negative net wealth — no cushion at all Mass ECHOS, p. 12
$10,000 total net wealth at the 25th percentile of Massachusetts families Mass ECHOS, p. 3

Why a wealth lens changes the picture

Statewide median family net wealth is $374,000, but the mean is $1,010,200 — which the report notes is more than 2.5 times the median — a gap that signals how concentrated wealth is, and how many families sit far below the average. Mass ECHOS, p. 12

The report explains that income alone does not capture a family's economic position. Wealth does two things income cannot: it buffers financial shocks, and it serves an investment function — enabling a down payment, a small business, a retirement nest egg, or paying for education. Absent savings, families often borrow at added cost, which can erode wealth further. Mass ECHOS, pp. 6–7

Everything below follows from this: what families need to build alongside housing stability, and where the evidence points.

What the data tells us

Median family net wealth differs substantially by educational attainment, rising at every step of the ladder — from $1,100 for families without a high-school diploma to $947,400 for those with a graduate degree. The report's appendix shows the same gradient in the ability to absorb a $400 shock. These are descriptive differences, not proof of cause; the report is explicit that it describes differences without offering causal explanations. Mass ECHOS, pp. 16–17

Connection to Education & Career Mobility: this is the evidence base for treating education as a pathway to economic mobility — higher attainment is associated with higher income and with substantially greater long-term financial security.

Possible language & sample paragraph

For a Statement of Need: "Mass ECHOS estimates show a substantial difference in median family wealth by educational attainment: $455,000 among families where someone holds a bachelor's degree, compared with $1,100 among families whose highest attainment is less than a high school diploma (Mattingly et al., 2026, pp. 16–17)."

One Family Scholars addresses this gap directly, pairing single parents with coaching, scholarships, and connections to resources to complete college degrees — attainment the survey associates with higher income and greater long-term financial security. draft — adapt as needed

Building Capacity · Core evidence

Financial Resilience & Asset Building

Savings, financial stability, and assets — the cushion that lets a family absorb a shock without falling into crisis, and the base from which longer-term assets grow.

The evidence

35.4% of families can't cover a $400 emergency expense Mass ECHOS, p. 20
39.9% have no retirement savings of any kind Mass ECHOS, p. 15
$20,000 median liquid assets — among the families who hold any at all Mass ECHOS, p. 18

Renters have even less of a cushion against a housing shock: 63.8% could not cover the roughly $9,600 cost of moving to a new apartment — evidence explored under Housing Security. Mass ECHOS, p. 15

What the data tells us

These figures describe a widespread shortfall in financial resilience. More than a third of Massachusetts families lack the savings to absorb a routine $400 emergency, and roughly two in five have no retirement assets building toward the future. A family without that financial cushion must borrow when the unexpected happens — borrowing that, as the report notes, can erode wealth further.

Connection to Financial Resilience & Asset Building: the gap is not only income — it is the missing buffer of savings and assets. This is why building financial resilience matters alongside, and beyond, raising income.

Possible language & sample paragraph

For a Statement of Need: "Renter families in Massachusetts hold almost no financial cushion — a median net wealth of $1,500, with nearly 40% in zero or negative territory (Mattingly et al., 2026, p. 17)."

One Family's Family Self-Sufficiency program pairs coaching with escrow savings accounts that grow alongside participant income. When 30% of FSS graduates leave with $10,000 or more in savings, that is a meaningful cushion for families whose peers often hold zero or negative net wealth — savings that can fund a security deposit, cover an emergency, pay down debt, or seed longer-term goals. (For context, in Gateway Cities median net financial wealth is just $1,100 — pp. 23–24.) draft — adapt as needed

Source · Mass ECHOS, pp. 15, 17, 18, 23–24

Expanding Possibility

Expanding Possibility · The bridge

Economic Mobility

Increased ability to pursue participant-defined goals and improve long-term economic security. Economic mobility is where capacity becomes movement — and it takes different forms for different families.

Economic Mobility is less a single statistic than the space between the other outcomes. Mass ECHOS shows how far apart families sit along the wealth continuum: from $10,000 at the 25th percentile to a $374,000 statewide median pp. 3, 12 — and the education, savings, and housing gaps documented in the surrounding sections are the rungs a family moves across.

Connection to Economic Mobility: the evidence here is directional, not a single headline number. Progress can mean higher income, reduced debt, growing savings, a degree, or a first asset — whichever goal a family defines. One Family measures movement along that continuum, in the direction each family chooses.

Source · Mass ECHOS, pp. 3, 12 · framing from surrounding themes

Building Security

Building Security · Core evidence

Housing Security

The ability to afford and sustain safe, secure housing, with reduced risk of future housing crisis — the foundation for economic security, and a place where assets can eventually be built.

The evidence

63.8% of renter families could not cover the estimated ~$9,600 cost of moving to a new apartment Mass ECHOS, p. 15
$1,500 median net wealth for renter families Mass ECHOS, p. 17
39.9% of renter families hold zero or negative net wealth Mass ECHOS, p. 17
−$3,200 net wealth for renters at the 25th percentile Mass ECHOS, p. 17

For contextual comparison, homeowning families held a median net wealth of $790,500, versus $1,500 for renters. Mass ECHOS, p. 17

What the data tells us

By the report's own benchmark, many renter families are far from housing security: 63.8% could not cover the roughly $9,600 it takes to move, so a rent increase or a lease ending can become a housing crisis. Renters hold almost no financial cushion, and at the lower end of the distribution they carry negative net wealth. The renter–homeowner wealth gap is large — though the report also notes Massachusetts ranked third nationally for median home value while posting the fifth-lowest homeownership rate, so high prices make buying difficult. Mass ECHOS, p. 22

Connection to Housing Security: the renter/homeowner comparison is evidence about financial capacity and cushion, not a verdict on tenure. Homeownership is one potential asset-building pathway, not the definition of success — the goal is the ability to afford and sustain secure housing, whatever form that takes for a family.

Possible language

For a Statement of Need / comms: "In Massachusetts, renter families hold a median net wealth of just $1,500, compared with $790,500 for homeowning families, and nearly 40% of renters have zero or negative net wealth — leaving them without a financial cushion against displacement (Mattingly et al., 2026, p. 17). Housing security means the ability to afford and sustain that housing, not homeownership alone."

Source · Mass ECHOS, pp. 15, 17, 22

Looking Across Generations

Looking Across Generations · Contextual evidence

Intergenerational Opportunity

Greater economic security and opportunity for children — the extent to which advantages, or disadvantages, carry from one generation to the next.

The evidence

13.4% of Massachusetts families received an inheritance or gift Mass ECHOS, p. 24
33.2% have a will or estate plan in place Mass ECHOS, p. 24

What the data tells us — read with care

These two figures are context, not proof of a full intergenerational pathway. Only about one in eight families received transferred wealth, and only a third have the estate planning that helps preserve and pass on what they have. Read together, they suggest that the transfer and preservation of wealth across generations is uneven — many families are building without an inherited head start, and without the tools to pass gains forward.

Connection to Intergenerational Opportunity: for families building wealth from a low base, the wealth they accumulate is often the first in their family line — which is precisely why the foundation One Family helps build carries into the next generation.

Source · Mass ECHOS, p. 24

Systems level · A parallel track

Systems Change

Policies and practices that reduce structural barriers to housing stability and economic mobility — change that benefits families beyond those One Family serves directly.

The evidence

White families $549,200
Black families $7,800
Hispanic families $1,200

Median net wealth by race & ethnicity · Mass ECHOS, p. 17

These gaps persist across the whole distribution. Even at the 75th percentile of the wealth distribution, white families hold $1,318,500, compared with $267,300 for Black families and $168,000 for Hispanic families. Mass ECHOS, p. 17

What the data tells us

These are descriptive disparities, not a specific causal mechanism. But their scale and their persistence across the entire distribution provide important context for understanding unequal economic opportunity and the structural barriers that systems-level work addresses. The report itself calls on public, private, and nonprofit actors to help build wealth and "expand participation in the economy." Mass ECHOS, p. 26

Connection to Systems Change: disparities this structural are not solved family by family alone. One Family engages the families most affected as active voices in shaping housing and economic policy — so that systemic solutions reflect the experience of those navigating the barriers firsthand.

Possible language & sample paragraph

For a Statement of Need: "Federal Reserve data show that racial wealth disparities in Massachusetts persist across the entire distribution: even at the 75th percentile of the wealth distribution, Black and Hispanic families hold a fraction of what white families hold (Mattingly et al., 2026, p. 17)."

The structural nature of wealth inequality demands policy-level solutions. One Family's advocacy engages the families most affected by these realities as active voices in shaping housing and economic policy — ensuring systemic solutions reflect the experiences of those navigating these barriers firsthand. draft — adapt as needed

Source · Mass ECHOS, pp. 17, 26

Using this data responsibly

  • Descriptive, not causal. Use "associated with" or "correlated with," never "caused by." pp. 5, 11
  • Confidence intervals exist for all estimates. For high-stakes claims, consider noting the range; some estimates are less precise. p. 11
  • Do not compare to other surveys. Methodology differs from the SCF and others. p. 11
  • "Family" ≠ Census "household." Mass ECHOS defines family by financial interdependence, not cohabitation. pp. 29–30
  • Collected March–December 2025, a period of market volatility; no timing adjustment was made. p. 25
  • Income data may skew low, which can affect wealth estimates. p. 25
  • Some group figures live in the appendix. Group-level shares for the $400 emergency question are in the report's Appendix Table 5, a separate data supplement. Figures marked † in this guide come from those appendix tables and were not re-verified against the report PDF on hand — confirm them before external use. p. 20; App. Table 5
